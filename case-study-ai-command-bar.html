Where it started

Every year, Hudl runs a skunkworks week: everyone on the product team gets a week to build whatever they want, no roadmap required. This one landed right at the start of AI tools becoming genuinely useful — just before most of us started reaching for them at work every day.

My coworker Gabby wanted to build an AI-powered search bar, mostly to learn how AI tools actually get built. I had a more specific reason to say yes: I already knew, from our upcoming Volleyball Consolidation effort, that we were going to need a search bar that could search and filter. Skunkworks gave us a week to find out if AI could do that well before we had to commit to it on a real roadmap.

Shipping out of a hackathon

We didn't just want a good demo for the skunkworks showcase — we wanted it live.

In one week, with seven engineers and two QA on the project, we built a working V1 of the command bar. That's an unusually large team for a skunkworks project, but it meant we could build something real instead of a throwaway prototype.

Because we already knew this was solving a problem we'd have to solve anyway, we made the case to our PM to skip the usual "skunkworks project quietly dies after the showcase" fate and ship V1 straight to production. We got the yes, and shipped it.

Eight months of listening before building more

Instead of jumping straight into a V2 roadmap, I spent about eight months on this as a side-of-desk project — watching the search prompts people actually typed into the command bar. Rather than guess what people wanted from an AI search tool, I let real usage tell me: what people searched for, where they got stuck, what phrasing they expected to work and didn't.

Prompt patterns and specific findings from this research are going into V2, below.

Where this is headed

This project is still active — V1 shipped, V2 is being scoped from eight months of real usage data, and V3 is already on the radar.
